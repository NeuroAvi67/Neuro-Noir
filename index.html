Daily Mystery

Why does the brain construct a subjective reality?

Your brain never touches the world directly. It receives electrical signals and builds a model: colors, sounds and a sense of “you” that exist nowhere outside your head. Why it feels like something to be that model is among the deepest open questions in science.

  1. Perception is controlled hallucination. The brain predicts what it expects to sense and uses incoming signals mainly to correct those predictions.
  2. Color is not out there. Light has wavelengths only; the experience of red is an invention of the visual system.
  3. The self is a model too. Body ownership and the feeling of being a unified “I” can be disrupted by simple illusions, like the rubber-hand effect.
Thought experiment

Mary’s Room. Mary knows every physical fact about color but has lived in black and white. When she first sees a red rose, does she learn something new? If yes, experience may be more than information.
